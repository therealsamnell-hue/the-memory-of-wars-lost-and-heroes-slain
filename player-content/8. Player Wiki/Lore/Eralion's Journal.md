_The DM apologizes for the state of fatigue and haste that led to the less-than-ideal prose. It has been edited and hopefully improved since initial posting._

Eralion's Journal is a well-worn, leatherbound volume. Its pages are yellowed and occasionally stained with ink, reagents, and the accumulated detritus of a wizard's working life. The entries are irregular, sometimes separated by months or even years, and vary considerably in tone. The earlier passages are written in an earnest hand. The later ones become increasingly assured, elaborate, and self-important. Together, they provide an intimate account of the wizard's life, ambitions, and eventual fate.

### Apprenticeship and Early Career

The earliest entries concern Eralion's youth in Sheirtalar and his apprenticeship under Vordal of the Emerald Rings, a dwarf of Clan Gordrivver. Vordal was already quite old when Eralion entered his service, well into his fourth century. The young apprentice writes about his master's various infirmities and declining vigor. More frequently, however, he mentions Vordal's profound sorrow over the state of the dwarven people. Their halls were emptying, their old glories fading, and Vordal seemed unable to speak of much else.

Eralion clearly felt considerable affection for his master, though this was occasionally tempered by frustration. In one entry, he complains of hearing the same lamentations for the hundredth time. He also writes of the difficulty of finding some fresh diversion to raise the old dwarf's spirits.

One of the few things that evidently pleased Vordal was Eralion's deep devotion to Moradin. The dwarf considered this an admirable quality in his pupil. His enthusiasm cooled considerably, however, whenever Eralion attributed his faith to some distant dwarven ancestry. This clearly became a sore point between them.

Eralion's apprenticeship began in the Year of the Dark Mask[^1]. The number of entries and their references to recent events suggest that it continued at least until the Year of Sinking Sails[^2].

The journal never records the formal conclusion of his apprenticeship. Several years after the last entry concerning Vordal, however, Eralion writes of returning to find that he had arrived too late. Age had finally claimed the elderly dwarf.

Eralion attempted to return Vordal's personal effects to Clan Gordrivver in person. He received an unexpectedly cold reception. Rather than admit him, the clan instructed him to deliver the belongings to a dwarven merchant. He was also warned, in the strongest possible terms, not to present himself at the gates of a Gordrivver dwarfhold, much less presume to ask for admittance.

The rejection clearly wounded him. Several subsequent entries dwell on religious speculation, questions of inheritance and belonging, and lengthy, rather overwrought prayers to Moradin. Eralion seems particularly troubled by the apparent contradiction between his master's affection for him and the contempt shown by his master's kin.

More than a decade passes before the next entry.

### Successful Adventurer

The Eralion who emerges from this gap is a very different man. He is confident, worldly, and firmly established in his profession. He records service with three different adventuring companies before eventually accumulating enough wealth to hire companions of his own. He finds this arrangement considerably more agreeable. It allows him to set the company's priorities without having to endure the competing ambitions and questionable judgment of others.

It is during this period that he records his first meeting with Feriblan of Kormul, on an expedition into the Channath Vale. Feriblan becomes a valued acquaintance, mentioned several times with evident fondness in later entries.

A recurring theme of this period is Eralion's dissatisfaction with his own accomplishments. By most standards, he is already a successful adventuring wizard. Yet he remains troubled by the feeling that he has not become a mage of sufficient renown to do justice to Vordal's memory. He frequently writes about the great works of legendary archmages and wonders whether his own name will ever be remembered alongside theirs.

Eventually, he accumulates enough wealth and materials to undertake a project of considerable ambition. After gathering an extraordinary quantity of reagents, he performs a grand working to summon a small host of earth elementals. He sets them to building a keep according to his own designs. The entire structure is raised in a single day and night.

Eralion is initially delighted with the result, but his satisfaction does not last. In an unusually candid observation, he admits that he probably spent as much ordinary Art correcting and refining the elementals' work as he would have spent supervising a more deliberate construction. He concludes, somewhat grudgingly, that patience might have served him better than ambition.

He names the keep Fairkeep and makes it his home. With his adventuring days behind him, he intends to devote himself more fully to the Art.

### Retirement and Romance

During the early years of his retirement, Eralion meets Gethrame. She becomes a frequent visitor to Fairkeep, and he makes regular journeys to see her in Drellin's Ferry. Their acquaintance begins with a shared interest in magical theory and religious philosophy. The journal records increasingly lengthy discussions between them, and Eralion seems delighted to have found someone with whom he can speak freely about such subjects.

Eventually, their affection grows beyond intellectual companionship. Eralion remarks that the details are properly the business of the watching gods. For a time, the entries suggest that he has found a happiness that neither wealth nor magical accomplishment had ever provided. His writing becomes less concerned with his reputation and more with the simple pleasures of companionship.

This period of contentment does not last.

The village priestess curses Gethrame for an alleged trespass involving scrying upon secret rites. Eralion insists that the offense was either entirely imaginary or far too trivial to justify the punishment. The priestess blinds Gethrame and casts her into exile.

Eralion is furious, but his initial attempts to discover what has happened to his beloved prove fruitless. She has fled the village without leaving any indication of where she has gone.

For more than a tenday, he writes nothing. When he finally resumes his journal, it is with barely concealed elation. Through divination, he has discovered Gethrame's whereabouts: a cave not far from Fairkeep. He goes to her immediately and promises to find a way to restore her sight. He also vows to accomplish this without placing his trust in the inconstant gods who have permitted such an injustice.

His subsequent entries describe a journey to Rethmar, where he contracts with an elven craftsman named Velior. Velior is commissioned to fashion a finely wrought staff capable of bearing considerable enchantment. Once the staff is complete, Eralion returns to Fairkeep and begins preparing the working that he hopes will restore Gethrame's vision.

One entry, in particular, is written with unusual vehemence:

_"I shall discard any question of prudence, in the face of manifest necessity and the compassion to which my love is so fulsomely due. The sacrifice required is from a vile creature, this scurrying, diseased shamaness. It is meet that she worships the Plague-Crone, for she imitates in her very nature that dark goddess."_

Gethrame is delighted with the new staff, which allows her to resume her scryings. Eralion shares her joy, but only for a time. He gradually becomes dismissive of her divinations, describing them as foolish soothsayings. He also begins to question her competence with the Art.

What had once been a shared fascination becomes, in his writing, an intolerable nuisance.

Eventually, he declares that he can no longer endure her company.

_"I have no choice but to leave her. I cannot stand to witness what she has become, nor hear again her senseless ramblings. Truly Savras must have blessed her in the same fashion as Chauntea did, only striking her wits instead of her eyes. I leave her to whatever her fate may be."_

There is no further mention of Gethrame.

### The Last Years

From this point onward, the journal becomes a rather different document. Eralion writes more frequently and with a growing sense of his own importance. His former references to Moradin, once so earnest and numerous, disappear entirely. In their place are increasingly elaborate meditations on the nature and limits of the Art, along with complaints about the injustice of mortal existence.

His chief grievance is now the brevity of his own life. He considers it absurd that a wizard of his considerable talents should be allotted so few years to pursue his studies when other practitioners of the Art have enjoyed centuries. The comparison with dwarves is particularly galling. Even a common dwarven blacksmith, he observes, may expect to outlive a human wizard of considerable accomplishment by generations. To Eralion, the unfairness of this arrangement is self-evident.

His initial researches into magic capable of extending his lifespan prove disappointing. He dismisses enchanted pools as likely to be the stuff of legend. He also regards elixirs of longevity as unreliable, despite having experimented with them in his youth. Nor does he possess the means to sustain an extended existence in some longer-lived form.

He briefly considers dedicating himself to Mystra, the Mother of All Magic, but ultimately rejects the idea. To him, it seems an unnecessarily elaborate way of surrendering his will and existence to a goddess in exchange for an eternity of toilsome service. 

One possibility, however, captures his attention. The so-called mushrooms of youth appear in several independent accounts. Some are sufficiently credible to attract his serious interest. These rare growths are said to occur only within the walls of [[The Tale of Rappan Athuk|Rappan Athuk]], the Dungeon of Graves.

In the Year of the Wandering Maiden[^3], Eralion makes the journey to that ill-starred place. He does not return with the mushrooms he sought. Instead, he comes back with discoveries that he considers considerably more valuable.

The journal does not fully explain the nature of these discoveries. The final entries do, however, make clear that Eralion believes he has found a means of transcending the ordinary limitations of mortal life.

The last entries are dated to the Year of the Weeping Moon[^4]. Eralion writes that he has sent a letter to his old acquaintance Feriblan of Kormul, telling him something of his discoveries. He intends to delay his final undertaking only a little longer. Partly, this is to ensure that all preparations are complete. Partly, it seems, he hopes Feriblan might arrive in time to witness his triumph.

The absence of a reply irritates him considerably. He speculates about the reasons for Feriblan's silence, but eventually dismisses the matter as unimportant. The work is ready, and he has no intention of allowing the indifference or tardiness of others to delay him any further.

His final entry is written in a tone of barely restrained excitement. He resolves to keep the journal close at hand so that he can record his sensations upon first awakening to his new and everlasting existence.
